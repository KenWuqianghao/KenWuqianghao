# Document ingestion, parsing and data-loading market (PDF→Markdown, web extraction, messy CSV) — as of late September 2026

Scope note: prices were fetched from vendor pricing pages on 2026-09-27 unless another date is given. Third-party aggregator numbers are flagged. Founder assets referenced: folio (CPU-only PDF→Markdown), marrow (web main-content extraction, WCXB test 0.8963), sieve (CSV dialect/header detection, Pollock).

## 1. Who are the paying competitors and what do they charge?

### Takeaway
PDF parsing APIs cluster at three price points: about $1–4 per 1,000 pages (commodity OCR/"fast" modes), $10–15 per 1,000 (layout/"balanced" parse: Reducto, Unstructured, Azure/Google layout, Datalab accurate) and $20–60 per 1,000 (extraction/agentic). Web extraction is cheaper still, at about $0.60–$3.20 per 1,000 pages. CSV importers are sold as SaaS seats/platform fees ($199–$799+/month, $5k–$15k+/yr), not per row. Every serious vendor reserves on-prem/VPC for "Enterprise, contact sales".

### Inferences
- The "layout parse" price floor from hyperscalers ($10/1k) matches Reducto's Parse and Datalab accurate. Unstructured sits at $15/1k. Vendors are converging on about 1–1.5¢/page for structured Markdown, and charge more only for LLM extraction and agentic passes.
- Commodity text-layer and OCR output is already $0.60–$4 per 1k (Textract/Azure Read, Mistral batch, LlamaParse Fast). A CPU-only parser that does not OCR scans competes at the bottom of that range on price, and must win on structure (tables and headings), not cost alone.
- Web extraction is already cheap per page. Buyers pay Firecrawl and Apify for crawling, anti-bot measures, JS rendering and proxies, not for main-content extraction quality. A better extractor such as marrow is a component, not a product, unless it is bundled with fetching.
- CSV importers monetize the *embedded UX* (column mapping, validation UI, white-label), not dialect detection. sieve alone addresses a sub-problem buyers do not see as a line item.

### Gaps
- Google Document AI's official page did not render in the fetch tool, so its prices are from aggregators (July 2026).

- Reducto Growth/Enterprise and Unstructured Business contract sizes are not public (beyond "seven-figure" for one Fortune 10 account).
- The Jina per-token price conflicts ($0.02 vs $0.05 per 1M tokens).

## 3. Where are the unmet needs, and what do buyers complain about?

### Takeaway
Buyers repeatedly raise the same complaints: tables and multi-column layouts break, self-hosting needs GPUs and hits out-of-memory errors, re-parsing a corpus at 1–5¢/page is expensive, and cloud APIs cannot be used in regulated or air-gapped settings. Most vendors address air-gapped use only with enterprise-only, contact-sales containers.

### Cited Findings
- HN "Ask HN: What are you using to parse PDFs for RAG?": "most libraries are totally fine for simple tables, but there are a _ton_ of wild tables in PDFs out there which are barely human-readable". Users mention Adobe's "$25K upfront" and hidden fees on Azure DI. Self-hosting "requires managing GPU resources for OCR", and "out-of-memory errors" on long documents are common. Marker "handles most issues except tables"; Unstructured is "computationally expensive for OCR-heavy documents" — [HN 41072632](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=41072632) (thread dated July 2024, somewhat stale)
- Agentic parsers are reported to be about 20% more accurate on hard documents at about $0.03/page. The gap "nearly vanish[es] on clean digital PDFs" and widens on financial tables, multi-column layouts, scans and forms. "Re-parsing an entire corpus after discovering extraction errors often exceeds initial savings" — [Particula, May 2026](https://particula.tech/blog/document-parsing-rag-reducto-llamaparse-unstructured-docling)
- Multi-column layouts: LlamaParse can interleave text from adjacent columns — [Firecrawl blog](https://www.firecrawl.dev/blog/best-pdf-parsers) (a competitor's blog)
- Air-gapped demand: defense contractors in SCIFs, HIPAA health systems and finance need "AI that works with zero external connectivity". LlamaParse has no true on-prem, only VPC on Enterprise — [Vstorm](https://vstorm.co/llamaindex/top-10-document-parsing-services-for-rag-pipelines-and-llm-applications/), [TrueFoundry](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/air-gapped-ai-deploying-enterprise-llms-in-highly-regulated-industries). Developers are writing about parsing for air-gapped RAG with "no cloud, no JVM, no Python" — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/deeplab/parsing-documents-for-air-gapped-rag-no-cloud-no-jvm-no-python-3a13). Unstructured markets a dedicated on-prem/air-gapped offering — [Unstructured](https://unstructured.io/insights/on-prem-air-gapped-document-ai-regulated-industries)
- On-prem availability today: Reducto (Enterprise), Datalab (Enterprise, custom terms), Mistral OCR 4 (Enterprise container), Azure DI (disconnected containers, annual commitment), Jina/Elastic (On-Prem SKU since Aug 2026), Unstructured (bare metal or VPC on Business) — sources as in section 1.
- Speed and cost of OSS on CPU (opendataloader-bench, 200 docs, s/page): opendataloader-hybrid 0.907 overall at 0.463 s; docling 0.882 at 0.762 s; marker 0.861 at **53.9 s/page**; mineru 0.831 at 5.96 s; unstructured hi_res 0.841 at 3.0 s; nutrient 0.885 at 0.008 s; opendataloader local 0.831 at 0.015 s — [opendataloader-bench](https://github.com/opendataloader-project/opendataloader-bench)
- Ken's folio README reports 0.9235 overall at 0.026 s/page on a 4-core Xeon (full 200). **Caveat:** on the 40-document held-out split folio scores 0.8790 vs opendataloader-hybrid 0.8971 and docling 0.8692, and it trails on tables (0.7135 vs 0.8935) — local file /home/user/folio/README.md (not a web source; the results are Ken's own)
- Mistral itself warns that benchmark mismatches stem largely from "ground-truth errors, notation equivalence issues, and segmentation differences" and recommends evaluating "on your own documents" — [Mistral OCR 4](https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/)
- Microsoft bought Osmos to solve the "first-mile" problem of "ingesting, cleaning, and transforming messy external data" — [Neowin](https://www.neowin.net/news/microsoft-acquires-osmos-to-bring-autonomous-ai-data-engineering-to-fabric/)

### Inferences
- The most defensible gap for folio: **fast, deterministic, CPU-only, air-gapped structured parsing of born-digital PDFs**. That means no GPU, no JVM, no data egress, a small container, and throughput 20–2,000× that of Marker or MinerU on the same hardware. The weak spots are scanned documents (no OCR) and hard tables, where agentic and VLM parsers justify their cost.
- A realistic product pattern is a **router**: folio handles the ~majority of clean digital pages for almost nothing and escalates hard pages (scans, dense tables) to a VLM. That attacks the "20% gap costs 3¢/page on every page" complaint.
- Hancom's OpenDataLoader is now also Apache-2.0, CPU-capable and on-prem, and it markets itself on the same benchmark. Topping that benchmark is a credible marketing hook, but not a moat. Held-out results show folio does not dominate on tables.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic G2 review data for Reducto, Unstructured or LlamaParse complaints, and no fresh (2026) HN or Reddit threads were fetched. The HN evidence dates from 2024.
- There is no public data on what share of enterprise PDFs are born-digital vs scanned (this matters for sizing a no-OCR CPU parser).
- There is no public data on how many buyers truly require air-gapped vs VPC deployment.

## 4. Do OSS maintainers in this space make money, and how?

### Takeaway
Yes, but mostly through **open-weights/open-code plus revenue-gated commercial licenses plus a hosted API** (Datalab), **AGPL plus cloud** (Firecrawl), **OSS library plus paid platform/VPC** (Unstructured), or corporate backing (Docling/IBM, OpenDataLoader/Hancom, Jina→Elastic on-prem SKU). Pure permissive-license solo maintainers such as trafilatura show no visible monetization.

### Cited Findings
- **Datalab/Marker**: code is Apache-2.0 (current README); model weights use a modified AI Pubs OpenRAIL-M license, free for research, personal use and startups under a revenue/funding threshold; above that, a paid license is required — [Marker GitHub](https://github.com/datalab-to/marker). *Conflict on the threshold:* the README says **$5M** funding/revenue; the Datalab pricing page says **$2M** — [Datalab pricing](https://www.datalab.to/pricing); Forkable (June 2025) says $2M and describes code as GPL at that time — [Forkable](https://www.forkable.io/p/datalab-develops-small-ai-models). Enterprise (API or on-prem licensing fees covering "overhead, custom work, and priority support") is the "largest revenue generator" — [Forkable](https://www.forkable.io/p/datalab-develops-small-ai-models). Result: seven-figure ARR with 7 people — [IDP-Software](https://idp-software.com/vendors/datalab/)
- **Firecrawl**: AGPL-3.0 core (SDKs MIT), self-hostable; the cloud has extra features; ~185.5k stars — [GitHub](https://github.com/firecrawl/firecrawl). It monetizes through the hosted credit plans in section 1 and raised $75M+ — [Runtimewire](https://runtimewire.com/article/firecrawl-raises-75m-series-b-alexandria-ai-agent-data)

- **Docling**: IBM Research project donated to the Linux Foundation AI & Data (Apr 2025) — [LF AI & Data](https://lfaidata.foundation/blog/2025/04/09/ibm-contributes-key-open-source-projects-to-linux-foundation-to-advance-ai-community-participation/). In 2026 ABBYY, IBM and Red Hat launched "DocLang", an open document-format standard under LF AI & Data — [OpenSourceForU](https://www.opensourceforu.com/2026/04/abbyy-ibm-red-hat-push-open-source-ai-document-standard/). I found no standalone Docling commercial SKU; monetization appears indirect (IBM watsonx and Red Hat AI).
- **trafilatura** (Adrien Barbaresi): dual MIT/Apache-2.0, widely used by "the AI, LLM and RAG industry". No evidence of sponsorship, a hosted API or a support business was found — [GitHub](https://github.com/adbar/trafilatura), [barbaresi.eu](https://adrien.barbaresi.eu/). Third parties such as Contextractor wrap it commercially — [Contextractor](https://www.contextractor.com/trafilatura/)
- **Jina Reader**: free hosted API with a token-billed paid tier. Since 2026-08-10 Elastic sells a "Jina On-Prem" commercial license for self-contained offline Docker containers — [Jina Reader](https://jina.ai/reader/)
- **Hancom OpenDataLoader PDF**: switched to Apache-2.0 in v2.0 (Mar 2026). Monetization is presumably through Hancom's broader products; no pricing was found — [PDF Association](https://pdfa.org/opendataloader-pdf-v20-tops-open-source-pdf-benchmarks-in-pdf-data-loading/)

### Inferences
- The Datalab playbook fits folio best: a permissive or source-available engine, a free tier for small companies, and a paid commercial/on-prem license for companies above a revenue threshold, plus a hosted API. For a CPU-only heuristic engine with no model weights, a revenue-gated license would have to sit on the code itself (e.g. BSL/Elastic-style or AGPL + commercial exception), which costs adoption.
- marrow and sieve look like trafilatura and CleverCSV: high-value components with no evident standalone monetization. They are best used as distribution and credibility for a paid ingestion product, or bundled into it.

### Gaps
- No revenue data for Hancom's OpenDataLoader, IBM Docling or trafilatura.
- No published Datalab commercial license prices.

## 5. What could a new entrant realistically charge? Would a CPU-only, self-hosted, cheap-per-page position work?

### Takeaway
A hosted per-page API for clean PDFs would be squeezed between free OSS (Docling, OpenDataLoader) and $1–4/1k commodity APIs (LlamaParse Fast, Mistral batch, Datalab fast), so it is hard to charge more than about $1–3 per 1k pages. The more realistic wedge is an **annual self-hosted or air-gapped license** priced below Azure disconnected containers and "contact sales" enterprise tiers: roughly $10k–$50k/yr per deployment for mid-market regulated buyers. Treat that range as inference, not a sourced benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- Price anchors buyers already see: Datalab fast $4/1k and Team $400/month — [Datalab](https://www.datalab.to/pricing); LlamaParse Fast $1.25/1k — [LlamaParse](https://developers.llamaindex.ai/llamaparse/general/pricing/); Mistral OCR 4 batch $2/1k — [Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/); Azure/Google layout $10/1k — [DocuOCR](https://docuocr.com/blog/azure-document-intelligence-pricing); Reducto Parse $10/1k — [Reducto](https://reducto.ai/pricing); Unstructured $15/1k — [Unstructured](https://unstructured.io/pricing)
- Air-gapped anchor: an Azure DI disconnected container costs 12× the monthly connected fee per year, e.g. **$8,640/yr for prebuilt at 100K pages/month**. Disconnected tiers start at 1.2M annual pages — [DocuOCR](https://docuocr.com/blog/azure-document-intelligence-pricing), [Azure](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/pricing/details/ai-document-intelligence/)
- Premium data importers charge $199–$799/month (CSVBox, Dromo Pro, Flatfile Professional) — section 1 sources
- Web extraction is already about $0.60/1k at Firecrawl Scale — [Firecrawl](https://www.firecrawl.dev/pricing)
- folio throughput is about 0.026 s/page on 4 cores (Ken's README), vs Marker 53.9 s/page and docling 0.76 s/page on the benchmark's hardware — [opendataloader-bench](https://github.com/opendataloader-project/opendataloader-bench)

### Inferences
- **Compute cost:** 0.026 s/page × 4 cores ≈ 0.1 core-seconds/page, so about 29 core-hours per 1M pages. At commodity cloud CPU prices, that is on the order of **a few dollars per million pages**. This is an estimate: I did not source a vCPU-hour price. Gross margin is therefore near 100% at any price above about $0.10/1k. Marginal cost is not the constraint; differentiation and trust are.
- **What works:** (a) a self-hosted/air-gapped license for regulated mid-market buyers (law firms, insurers, regional banks, health systems, government contractors) who cannot send documents to Reducto or LlamaParse and find Azure containers or "contact sales" enterprise tiers heavy. (b) An "escalation router" that is cheap by default and sends only hard pages to a VLM. (c) Positioning as the pre-filter or first pass for existing parser vendors or RAG platforms, i.e. an OEM license.
- **What probably will not work:** a generic hosted cheap-per-page API. Mistral, LlamaParse Fast and free OSS already occupy it, and CPU-only means no OCR for scans, which are a large share of the insurance, healthcare and government documents that pay most.
- **Honest crowding assessment:** the PDF→Markdown space is very crowded. It has at least 6 well-funded APIs, 3 hyperscalers, 2 LLM labs selling OCR, and at least 5 strong OSS engines, 2 of them corporate-backed (Docling/IBM, OpenDataLoader/Hancom). Web extraction is dominated by Firecrawl's distribution (~185k stars, 1.25M developers). CSV importing has consolidated. A solo entrant wins only in a narrow wedge: CPU/air-gapped, speed, determinism and auditability, or a vertical.

### Gaps
- No public sources give prices for comparable self-hosted parser licenses (Datalab and Reducto on-prem are unpublished), so the $10k–$50k/yr range is an inference.
- No survey data on buyer willingness to pay for CPU-only or no-GPU deployments.

## 6. Which vertical niches pay most for ingestion?

### Takeaway
BFSI (banking, financial services, insurance) is the largest paying vertical, at about 30–33% of IDP spend. Legal and finance research (Harvey, Hebbia, Rogo) are the marquee AI-native buyers of premium parsing. Healthcare, insurance and government/defense drive the on-prem and air-gapped requirement.

### Cited Findings
- BFSI is projected at a 32.7% share of IDP in 2026 (loan processing, underwriting, claims, KYC, financial statements). The finance & accounting function accounts for 45.57% of the market — [search summary of Fortune Business Insights / Mordor / Precedence](https://www.fortunebusinessinsights.com/intelligent-document-processing-market-108590)
- Reducto's named buyers: Harvey (legal), Rogo (finance), Mercor, a top-5 hedge fund, a Fortune 10 company, and "category leaders in Healthcare, Insurance, and Real Estate" — [Reducto](https://reducto.ai/blog/reducto-series-b-funding)
- Reducto's target verticals represent "over 50% of US GDP" — [Contrary](https://research.contrary.com/company/reducto)
- Instabase ($1.2B valuation) serves financial services, insurance, compliance and HR — [Tracxn](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/instabase/__Ejmst0iD15QNDlTKh5GokuQUh4tjjgysJKOdpQduAzk). Hebbia serves asset managers, investment banks and law firms — [Hebbia Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebbia)
- Air-gapped needs are concentrated in defense (SCIFs), HIPAA healthcare and regulated finance — [TrueFoundry](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/air-gapped-ai-deploying-enterprise-llms-in-highly-regulated-industries)
- Particula recommends Reducto for regulated finance and legal (on-prem, SOC 2 Type II, HIPAA, ZDR) — [Particula](https://particula.tech/blog/document-parsing-rag-reducto-llamaparse-unstructured-docling)
- Datalab's biggest named customers are frontier AI labs (Anthropic) — [IDP-Software](https://idp-software.com/vendors/datalab/). AI labs and data vendors buy parsing at massive page volumes for training-data pipelines.

### Inferences
- For a CPU-only, born-digital-strong parser, the best-fit verticals are **finance** (10-K/10-Q filings, prospectuses, broker research: mostly born-digital, table-heavy, high volume) and **legal** (contracts and filings: mostly born-digital, confidentiality-sensitive). Insurance and healthcare pay well but skew toward scanned forms and handwriting, which need OCR/VLM.
- **AI labs and data vendors** (e.g. Mercor-type data companies) are a distinct high-volume buyer. At billions of pages, CPU cost per page and throughput matter a lot, and Datalab's traction shows they pay. Ken's employer relationship could create a conflict of interest or IP question, and he should check his employment agreement before selling to this segment.
- **Government/defense** values air-gapped, no-GPU deployment most, but procurement is slow and hard for a solo founder.

### Gaps
- No sourced per-vertical price points (e.g. what law firms pay per page vs insurers).
- No data on the born-digital vs scanned mix by vertical.
