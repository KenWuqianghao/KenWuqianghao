# Sell the parser, rent the method

Ken Wu's most sellable asset is **folio, packaged as a self-hosted, CPU-only document-ingestion licence** for regulated finance and legal teams, run on the open-core model that took Datalab to **seven-figure ARR with seven people** ([IDP-Software](https://idp-software.com/vendors/datalab/)). The fastest cash is a **fixed-price, leak-checked applied-ML sprint** built on reldfs and the research loop: a $5–10k diagnostic that leads into a $15–30k sprint. The other assets (tally, sieve, marrow, streetwise, tremor, quill, the TGB heuristics and weave) are real technical wins. Most of them, though, land in markets where the technique is given away free (log templating, address parsing, spelling), where the category is consolidating (CSV import, relational AutoML), or where sales take 6–18 months (warehouse robotics). There they work better as components, lead magnets or credibility than as standalone companies. The biggest constraint is Mercor, not the market. Mercor's core business is custom evals, benchmarks and RL environments for AI labs ([Mercor](https://www.mercor.com/blog/welcome-to-the-era-of-evals/)), so the most lucrative use of Ken's research loop, selling that work to labs, is the one idea he should not pursue while employed there. Everything below is ranked on market pull, head start, solo feasibility and defensibility as of September 2026, then scored separately for employment risk. The report ends with a two-track recommendation and a day-by-day first week.

Three patterns follow from this. First, **the wins are CPU-cheap, deterministic and dependency-light**. That fits self-hosted, air-gapped and cost-sensitive buyers, not "best accuracy at any price" buyers. Second, the honest caveats (folio's tables, tally's held-out failures, marrow's non-significant margin, tremor's multivariate track) are exactly what a technical buyer will test. Publishing them first builds the trust that open-source vendors live on. Third, about half the assets are **quality improvements to free things**: Drain, libpostal, trafilatura, SymSpell. Grafana, Loki and the OpenTelemetry Collector are already absorbing log templating as a free feature ([OTel #47235](https://github.com/open-telemetry/opentelemetry-collector-contrib/issues/47235), [Grafana Adaptive Logs](https://grafana.com/docs/grafana-cloud/observe-and-act/adaptive-telemetry/adaptive-logs/introduction/)), and the Bing Spell Check API and Grammarly's developer SDK have both been shut down ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/announcements/bing-search-api-retirement), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/07/13/grammarly-to-shut-down-the-text-editor-sdk-in-january/)). Being better than a free default rarely creates a new invoice.

## Mercor's business sets the boundary before the market does

*This is general guidance, not legal advice. The operative document is Ken's Mercor offer letter and its proprietary-information and invention-assignment agreement (PIIA), which are not public.*

Mercor is not a neutral employer for these ideas. It reached about **$2B in annualized gross revenue in June 2026** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/mercor/)). It sells the APEX benchmarks, custom evals and "environment generation using autograders" to labs and enterprises ([Mercor](https://www.mercor.com/blog/welcome-to-the-era-of-evals/)), and it bought Sepal AI and Deeptune in 2026 to build out RL environments ([RL List](https://www.rl-list.com/vendors/mercor)). It is also a **named Reducto customer**, so it buys document parsing ([Reducto](https://reducto.ai/blog/reducto-series-b-funding)). Two legal defaults apply, and both give way to the contract. In Ontario there is no statute on employee inventions. Under *Comstock Canada v. Electec* (1991), an employee not hired to invent kept an invention made on his own time, absent a contractual restriction ([CanLII Connects](https://canliiconnects.org/en/commentaries/28950)). California Labor Code §2870 protects inventions made on the employee's own time without employer resources, **but only if they do not relate to the employer's current or prospective business** ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/trade-secrets/1778072/california-labor-code-2870-employee-invention-ownership-guide)). Courts have ruled against founders who had the right facts but no contemporaneous evidence, such as time logs and proof of personal hardware. The standard advice is to read the moonlighting policy before incorporating and to get any required pre-approval in writing ([Wilson Sonsini](https://ecvc.wsgr.com/faq/formation/when-to-start/im-currently-employed-can-i-work-on-my-startup), [Promise Legal](https://blog.promise.legal/employer-ip-invention-assignment-startup-founders/)).

That gives a clear gradient. **Red:** selling evals, benchmark construction, RL environments or expert data to AI labs competes head-on with Mercor's fastest-growing line and its customers. **Amber:** eval-integrity audits for enterprises sit next to Mercor's enterprise evals. Document ingestion is also amber, not because Mercor sells parsing but because Mercor buys it: Ken should never sell to Mercor, and should never build on anything he learns about Mercor's pipelines. **Green:** vertical tools for bookkeepers, log-cost work for SRE teams, predictive models on a client's own warehouse, address and geocoding tools, and robotics planning are all far from Mercor's business. Even green work needs written approval if the contract requires outside-activity disclosure. Time is a constraint in its own right: a founding-stage MTS role leaves little of it, and services revenue scales only with hours.

Four practical steps reduce the risk for every idea. First, list every asset in the PIIA's prior-inventions schedule, with repository dates and commit hashes: folio, sieve, quill, tally, marrow, streetwise, tremor, reldfs, the TGB code, weave and the loop itself. Second, keep all side work on personal hardware and accounts, outside Mercor hours. Third, exclude AI labs and Mercor's customers from any client list. Fourth, spend an hour with an Ontario employment/IP lawyer before taking money. There is also an upside: the research loop's highest-value application while he is employed is probably *inside* Mercor, where anti-reward-hacking and verifiability are what lab buyers value most ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/state-of-rl-envs)).

### 1. Leak-checked applied-ML sprints turn reldfs into cash in weeks

**Customer and painful job.** The customer is the head of data or growth at a mid-market SaaS, fintech or e-commerce company whose data sits in Snowflake, BigQuery or Postgres. The job is "tell me which customers will churn, convert or default, and prove the lift is real". reldfs fits it directly: it beat RelBench's GNN baselines on CPU with explainable features. The loop's held-out gates answer the question buyers rarely get answered honestly: will this gain survive next quarter's data? **Pricing.** A paid **$5–10k one-week diagnostic** builds a leak-checked harness on the client's tables and reports the baseline and headroom. It leads into a **$15–30k, 2–3 week sprint** against a stated metric, then an optional retainer. The anchors: Toptal ML engineers bill **$150–250/hour** ([Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/developers/machine-learning)); productized AI sprints list at **$15–25k for 2–3 weeks** ([AIDOLS](https://aidolsgroup.com/en/pricing/)); a 2-week AI audit lists at $15k ([ConsultKit](https://www.consultkit.ai/blog/how-to-productize-ai-consulting-services)). Paid audits convert to implementation work better than free ones ([Dojo Labs](https://dojolabs.co/blog/ai-consulting-pricing-models/)). **Competitors.** Self-serve Pecan costs **~$760–1,400/month** ([Pecan](https://www.pecan.ai/pricing/)). Enterprise AutoML starts at ~$60–120k/yr for H2O and ~$75k/yr for DataRobot (aggregator figures: [cloudpricecalculators](https://cloudpricecalculators.com/h2o-ai-calculator/), [Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/datarobot)). Kumo, the category's flagship, now sits inside Nvidia as a NIM ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/03/nvidia-snaps-up-kumo-ai-in-latest-acquisition/)). That leaves "Kumo-class accuracy, CPU-only, you own the model" as a clean pitch.

**First 10 customers.** Publish reldfs with the RelBench submission and a write-up titled something like "beating GNNs on RelBench with LightGBM on a laptop". Post it where data leads read it. Then offer the fixed-price diagnostic directly to 30 warm contacts (UWaterloo alumni founders, co-op employers) and to dbt/Snowflake community members, never to AI labs or Mercor customers. **90 days.** Weeks 1–2: get written Mercor approval, publish the repo and submit the RelBench entry. Weeks 3–6: sell and deliver two discounted diagnostics as case studies. Weeks 7–12: convert one to a full sprint and publish an anonymized case study with before-and-after held-out numbers. **Risks.** Revenue is capped by evening hours. Niche leaderboard gains are weak evidence of client ROI. Client data access also creates confidentiality obligations that must be kept apart from Mercor work.

### 2. folio Enterprise sells what regulated buyers cannot get off the shelf

**Customer and painful job.** The customers are platform or AI teams at mid-market banks, asset managers, insurers' finance functions and law firms. They must turn large volumes of born-digital PDFs (filings, prospectuses, contracts) into structured Markdown for retrieval and extraction, **without data leaving their network and without GPUs**. Air-gapped demand clusters in defense, HIPAA healthcare and regulated finance ([TrueFoundry](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/air-gapped-ai-deploying-enterprise-llms-in-highly-regulated-industries)). Today that need is served mainly by enterprise-only "contact sales" containers. LlamaParse has no true on-prem option ([Vstorm](https://vstorm.co/llamaindex/top-10-document-parsing-services-for-rag-pipelines-and-llm-applications/)). folio's differentiator is throughput: 0.026 s/page against docling's 0.762 and marker's **53.9 s/page** ([opendataloader-bench](https://github.com/opendataloader-project/opendataloader-bench)). By Ken's own estimate that means compute costs of a few dollars per million pages.

**Pricing and competitors.** The licence would be priced at roughly **$10–50k/yr per deployment**. That range is an inference, because Reducto and Datalab on-prem prices are unpublished. The public anchors are these. Azure Document Intelligence **disconnected containers cost 12× the monthly fee per year, e.g. $8,640/yr for prebuilt at 100K pages/month** ([DocuOCR](https://docuocr.com/blog/azure-document-intelligence-pricing)). Reducto charges **$10 per 1,000 pages** for Parse, with VPC/on-prem only at Enterprise ([Reducto](https://reducto.ai/pricing)). Unstructured charges **$15/1k** ([Unstructured](https://unstructured.io/pricing)). Datalab's Team plan costs **$400/month** and its accurate mode $10/1k ([Datalab](https://www.datalab.to/pricing)). Mistral OCR 4 costs $4/1k, with self-hosting via sales ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/)). The free competitors are the dangerous ones: Docling (MIT, Linux Foundation) and **Hancom's OpenDataLoader PDF v2.0, Apache-2.0, on-prem, with free OCR and table add-ons**, which markets itself on the same benchmark folio tops ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/hancom-tops-open-source-pdf-benchmarks-with-opendataloader-pdf-v2-0--302713099.html)). The model to copy is Datalab's: open code, a hosted API, and paid commercial and on-prem licences as its "largest revenue generator" ([Forkable](https://www.forkable.io/p/datalab-develops-small-ai-models)). Because folio is already Apache-2.0, the paid tier has to bundle things the core lacks: a hardened container, a support SLA, a table-quality add-on, a VLM escalation hook for scanned pages, audit logging and indemnity.

**First 10 customers.** Launch on Hacker News with the full truth, held-out table numbers included. Open the opendataloader-bench PR. Then work the issue trackers of Docling, Marker and OpenDataLoader for users asking about CPU-only or air-gapped use. Offer a paid "folio on your documents" diagnostic ($3–5k), which doubles as the sales motion. Target finance and legal first, because their documents are mostly born-digital; insurance and healthcare skew toward scans that folio cannot read. **90 days.** Month 1: publish foliopdf on PyPI with honest docs and a scoring CLI that runs on a buyer's own samples. Month 2: **point the research loop at held-out tables** (0.7135 vs hybrid's 0.8935), since tables are what finance buyers test first; ship the container and a scan-detection fallback. Month 3: close 2–3 design-partner licences at a founder discount. **Risks.** Corporate-backed free alternatives. No OCR. A table gap that shows up on real bank and filing documents. Regulated procurement cycles. The amber Mercor adjacency: Ken should never sell to Mercor or to AI labs, and should record folio as a prior invention.

### 7. A parse-router API is a real idea in the most crowded aisle

**Customer and painful job.** The customer is a RAG developer paying 1–5¢ a page to parse a whole corpus, when agentic parsers mostly beat cheap ones only on hard pages. Reported gaps "nearly vanish on clean digital PDFs", and re-parsing a whole corpus is costly ([Particula](https://particula.tech/blog/document-parsing-rag-reducto-llamaparse-unstructured-docling)). The router sends clean pages to folio for almost nothing and escalates scans and dense tables to a VLM. **Pricing.** About $1.50 per 1,000 clean pages, with escalated pages passed through at cost plus margin. **Competitors.** LlamaParse costs **$1.25–56.25/1k** by tier ([LlamaIndex](https://developers.llamaindex.ai/llamaparse/general/pricing/)), Datalab $4–10/1k, Mistral **$2/1k batch**, Reducto $10/1k, Unstructured $15/1k. Firecrawl's $75M Series B shows where the money flows: to distribution and agents, not to raw parsing ([Runtimewire](https://runtimewire.com/article/firecrawl-raises-75m-series-b-alexandria-ai-agent-data)). **Go-to-market and 90 days.** Treat it as the hosted tier of idea 2, not a separate company. Ship it in month 2 of the folio plan, with a pricing calculator showing the blended cost. **Risks.** Price compression from LLM vendors. Hosting sensitive documents means SOC 2 expectations. The best-funded competition in any of these ten ideas.

### 8. streetwise recovers failed geocodes, but parsing is not the invoice

**Customer and painful job.** The customer is a team that self-hosts Nominatim or Photon to avoid Google's per-call fees, licence terms or privacy exposure, and loses up to a third of messy addresses. On that data, streetwise lifts Nominatim's held-out match rate by 10.9 points. **Pricing.** OEM or support licences in the low four to low five figures a year (inference), or $99–499/month hosted. **Competitors.** Google Geocoding charges **$5 per 1,000** after 10k free, and Address Validation **$17 per 1,000** ([Google](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)). Smarty's annual plans run roughly $195–4,980 (aggregator, via [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10014524/Smarty/pricing/)). Placekey gives **10,000 queries a day free** ([Placekey](https://www.placekey.io/pricing)). libpostal is MIT and has a model refresh from Senzing ([Senzing](https://senzing.com/new-libpostal-data-model-from-senzing/)). **Honest market read:** buyers pay for verification against postal reference data, which streetwise lacks. A hosted libpostal API that once existed appears to be defunct. **Go-to-market and 90 days.** Contribute upstream to Nominatim and Photon, publish the MESSY STREETS results, and pitch entity-resolution and KYC teams that cannot send personal data to a SaaS. It works best folded into idea 5. **Risks.** Low willingness to pay, and free incumbents that are improving.

### 9. weave's route is a planner audit, then a clean-room SDK

**Customer and painful job.** The customers are vendor-agnostic fleet-management and orchestration companies (Meili, KINEXON, SyncroBot, Ati) and integrators running mixed AMR fleets. Top vendors such as Amazon, Geek+ and Symbotic build coordination in-house; Amazon now frames it as a foundation-model problem ([Amazon Science](https://www.amazon.science/blog/amazon-builds-first-foundation-model-for-multirobot-coordination)). **Pricing.** $10–50k planner audits (inference), then a source or SDK licence. Meili's "Code License" is the closest precedent, with an undisclosed price ([Automated Warehouse](https://www.automatedwarehouseonline.com/meili-robots-launches-code-license-for-its-fleet-management-system/)). **Blocking issue.** MAPF-LNS2 permits only "educational, research and non-profit purposes" without a USC Stevens licence ([MAPF-LNS2](https://github.com/Jiaoyang-Li/MAPF-LNS2)). The commercial base must therefore be a documented clean-room rewrite on MIT-licensed LaCAM3 ([LaCAM3](https://github.com/Kei18/lacam3)). **Go-to-market and 90 days.** Submit the 2,478 solutions with honest crediting, enter the League of Robot Runners (prizes are only $2.5–5k, but it is an Amazon-sponsored stage, and the 2026 edition added execution delays) ([LoRR 2026 CFP](https://groups.google.com/g/ai-robotics/c/BSUwmuf42Po)), and begin the clean-room rewrite. **Risks.** Offline best-known-solution gains on grid benchmarks do not transfer to lifelong, delay-robust industrial planning. Sales cycles run 6–18 months. It is a relationship business, not SaaS.

### 10. Evals and RL environments for labs pay the most and conflict the most

The market is real. Per-task RL-environment prices run **$200–2,000**, contracts run six to seven figures per quarter, and buyers rank robustness against reward hacking and verifiability first ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/gradient-updates/state-of-rl-envs)). **Assets that stay credibility, not products.** The TGB heuristics have no obvious direct buyer. Their value is as features inside fraud or recommendation work in idea 1, and as a research signal. quill's best commercial home is domain-lexicon correction, such as drug names, part numbers or OCR post-correction, inside the ingestion products. It is a small market: platform spelling APIs have been retired, and Sapling charges **$0.002 per 1,000 characters** ([Sapling](http://sapling.ai/docs/api/pricing/)). Its CC BY-SA model data also needs attribution in any OEM deal. marrow's statistically tied lead does not justify a company against Firecrawl's **1.25M developers** ([Runtimewire](https://runtimewire.com/article/firecrawl-raises-75m-series-b-alexandria-ai-agent-data)). It belongs as the HTML path of folio's ingestion stack.

## Conclusion

The benchmark portfolio does not point to one breakthrough company. It points to one marketable property shared by nearly every asset: **near-state-of-the-art quality on CPU, deterministic, with no data egress**. That property commands a premium in only a few places today: regulated document ingestion, observability bills, and client-owned predictive models. It is worth little where the default is already free and "good enough". So the bet is on the property rather than any single repo. folio is the best current carrier of it, and each other asset can join the same ingestion or applied-ML story as a component rather than competing for attention as its own startup.

The less obvious conclusion is that the research loop is worth more as a way to keep an edge than as a product. The loop is not unique: Weco, open-source agents and frontier coding tools all automate benchmark climbing. What competitors cannot copy cheaply is Ken's record of publishing held-out caveats alongside wins. In a market where Reducto is valued at roughly 120× revenue and parsers are converging on a cent a page, that trust is the moat. The first job is therefore to protect it on two fronts: keep the Mercor boundary clean on paper, and ship folio with its table gap stated before a buyer finds it.
